Ingredient Cost Helper: Buying in Bulk

Grocery items are almost never purchased in the exact amount a recipe calls for. Use this two-step method any time you only use part of what you bought.

Step 1 — Unit Cost = Purchase Price ÷ Purchase Quantity
Step 2 — Ingredient Cost = Unit Cost × Quantity Used in Recipe
Example: You buy a dozen (12) eggs for $3.60, but the recipe only uses 1 egg.
Step 1: $3.60 ÷ 12 eggs = $0.30 per egg (unit cost).
Step 2: $0.30 × 1 egg used = $0.30 — that's the number you enter in the "Cost for Qty" column below for that ingredient.
This same two-step method works for any bulk item: a bag of flour you only scoop 2 cups from, a bottle of oil you use 1/4 cup of, a bunch of celery you use 2 stalks from, etc. Just make sure the "purchase quantity" and "quantity used" are in the same unit (both eggs, both cups, both ounces, etc.) before you divide.

Part I: Determine Recipe Cost

Enter each recipe's original yield and ingredients. Costs scale automatically to the 50-serving target. Use the Ingredient Cost Helper above to figure out the "Cost for Qty" value for any ingredient you buy in bulk but only partially use.

Recipe (Total) Cost = ∑ (ingredient cost, scaled)
Portion Cost = Total Recipe Cost ÷ Servings (50)

Part II: Calculate Selling Price

Default desired food cost percentage is 33.3%, as specified in the assignment. You may adjust it if your instructor directs otherwise. Try calculating the selling price yourself first using the formula below, then enter your answer to check it before the correct value is revealed.

Portion Cost = Total Food Cost ÷ Servings
Selling Price = Food Cost (per portion) ÷ Desired Food Cost %
